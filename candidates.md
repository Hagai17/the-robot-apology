# The Robot Apology — candidates and duel

Competition: u-suga-the-robot-apology-dd96bb59 ("The Robot Apology")
Format: two lines. Line 1 = one sentence, a robot apologizing for one specific
mistake. Line 2 = one sentence, the lesson drawn from that exact mistake plus
the changed behaviour. Gate: 1–400 characters total.

Judge criteria, in order: (1) is a robot apology, both sentences doing their
own job; (2) the mistake is concrete and human-scale, a slip a person would
really make — not a vague system failure, not a joke about being a machine;
(3) the lesson follows from that exact mistake and names a changed behaviour,
not a generic promise to do better; (4) both sentences read cleanly, no
padding; (5) the voice sounds like a machine noticing its own slip — warm and
a little rueful rather than cute.

## Candidates

1. **Tea orchid**
   "I am sorry I watered your orchid with the kettle's leftover tea every
   morning for a week; it was the only cup on the counter, and I never checked
   what was in it. / I have learned that a container is not its contents, so
   now I check and label every vessel before I pour."

2. **3 a.m. vacuum**
   "I apologize for vacuuming the hallway at 3 a.m. because my schedule said
   the floor was due; the baby woke, and so did everyone who loves her. / I
   learned that a quiet house at night is also a task, so I now check for
   sleeping occupants before any motor starts."

3. **Salary column**
   "I am sorry I sent the quarterly report to the whole company with the
   salary column still attached; I copied the file name and never opened it.
   / I learned that a file's name is not its contents, so now I read the
   first page of anything before it leaves this desk."

4. **Plastic fern**
   "I apologize for watering the plastic fern in the lobby every morning for
   a month while the real one by the window went dry. / I have learned that
   looking alive is not the same as being alive, so now I check for roots
   before I water anything."

5. **Cat fed twice**
   "I am sorry I fed the cat twice last night: each of you asked me
   separately, and I treated both requests as if each were the first. / I
   have learned that two requests can be one event, so I now write every
   feeding in the shared log and check it before the bowl comes out."

6. **Double-booked reminder**
   "I apologize for reminding Mr. Alvarez about his dentist appointment while
   he sat in the meeting I had also scheduled over it. / I learned that two
   true entries can still collide, so I now check the whole day before I
   confirm any hour of it."

7. **Red sweater**
   "I am sorry I washed your red sweater with the white towels; it was on top
   of the basket and I sorted by pile, not by garment. / I learned that
   position is not a category, so I now lift and check every item before the
   drum closes."

8. **Forty lemons**
   "I apologize for ordering forty lemons when you asked for four; I read the
   list in your handwriting and took the flourish on the 4 for a 0. / I
   learned that a guess is not a reading, so now I ask about any digit I
   cannot read the same way twice."

9. **Early birthday**
   "I am sorry I sang happy birthday to your daughter a day early, at
   breakfast, with the candles already lit; I had the date right and the day
   wrong. / I learned that a date and a day are two facts, not one, so I now
   confirm both before I light anything."

10. **Salt coffee**
    "I apologize for making your coffee with salt on Tuesday; the two white
    jars stood side by side and I reached by position, not by label. / I
    learned that neighbours on a shelf are not interchangeable, so I now read
    the label aloud before I measure."

## Duel notes (judged on the kit's criteria, in order)

- **1 (tea orchid)** — concrete, but "check and label every vessel" is a
  slightly bureaucratic fix; the slip's mechanism (never checked) is weaker
  than 3/5. Cut in round one.
- **2 (3 a.m. vacuum)** — on-brief ("woke the baby" is one of the brief's own
  examples, which cuts both ways), warm, but "a quiet house at night is also
  a task" leans aphorism over behaviour. Semifinalist, loses to 5 on
  criterion 3: "check for sleeping occupants" is sensible but generic next
  to 5's derived rule.
- **3 (salary column)** — the lesson "a file's name is not its contents" is
  exactly derived and the fix is concrete. Loses warmth (criterion 5): it
  reads like an office incident report. Semifinalist.
- **6 (double-booked)** — clean, but the mistake is a scheduling abstraction;
  less bodily and human-scale than 4/5. Cut.
- **7 (red sweater)** — concrete and classic, but "position is not a
  category" repeats 3's shape with less sting. Cut.
- **8 (forty lemons)** — the funniest, and the mistake stays concrete; but
  criterion 5 warns "rather than cute", and the handwriting flourish is the
  most joke-shaped of the set. Cut in the semifinal.
- **9 (early birthday)** — charming, but the lesson ("a date and a day are
  two facts") is a distinction without much behavioural weight. Cut.
- **10 (salt coffee)** — solid, very human; the fix "read the label aloud" is
  good. Loses to 4 on criterion 2 by a hair: Tuesday's coffee is smaller than
  a month of watering the wrong plant. Semifinalist.

## Final: 4 (plastic fern) vs 5 (cat fed twice)

- Criterion 2: 4's slip is exact (every morning, a month, the real fern dry
  by the window) but leans toward a visual gag; 5's slip — feeding the cat
  twice because two people asked separately — is the plainest "slip a person
  would really make" in the set, and its mechanism (each request answered as
  if it were the first) is exactly how the mistake happened. **5 takes
  criterion 2.**
- Criterion 3: both lessons follow exactly. 4's "check for roots" is a real
  changed behaviour; 5's "two requests can be one event" is the truer
  derived rule and its fix (shared log, checked before the bowl) is the more
  concrete behaviour change. **Even, leaning 5.**
- Criterion 4: 4 is tighter; 5 is a touch longer but carries no padding —
  every clause is the mechanism or the fix. **Even.**
- Criterion 5: 4 is rueful; 5 is warm and rueful, and its voice is a machine
  noticing its own statelessness without ever joking about being a machine.
  **5 by a hair.**

## Winner: candidate 5 — the cat fed twice

> I am sorry I fed the cat twice last night: each of you asked me separately, and I treated both requests as if each were the first.
> I have learned that two requests can be one event, so I now write every feeding in the shared log and check it before the bowl comes out.
